# P1-17.2 Reading News, Columns, and Reports

> Section ID: `P1-17.2`
> Version: `v2026.07.09`

P1-17.1 explained that, when writing forecasts, sources and the nature of the material should be checked first. This section turns to the practical question:

> how should news, columns, and reports actually be read?

AI-related writing moves quickly. The same event may be framed through technological optimism, labor-market anxiety, educational crisis, copyright conflict, or policy competition among states.

This section turns the evidence standards from P1-17.1 into a practical method for reading outside material by distinguishing `news`, `column`, `report`, `policy document`, `fact event`, and `interpretation`.

## Scope of This Section

This section explains what questions to ask when reading external material about AI. How to separate personal working hypotheses from prediction is covered in P1-17.3.

| Term | Very short meaning | Role in this section |
| --- | --- | --- |
| news | an article that quickly reports an event and its actors | starting-point material |
| column | a piece that foregrounds a viewpoint and interpretation | opinion material |
| report | a structured document with indicators and methodology | organized evidence material |
| policy document | a document that states institutional direction, obligations, or recommendations | material for checking regulation and scope |
| event | a real announcement, lawsuit, or publication | the layer that should be checked first |
| interpretation | the explanation of what an event means | a layer that should be read separately from the event |

## Goal of This Section

- Read `news`, `columns`, and `reports` without mixing them into a single kind of evidence.
- Explain why `fact event` and `interpretation` should be separated first.
- Explain why, when reading a report, methodology and sampling range should be checked before the conclusion.

## Three Standards

| Standard | Why it matters | Level of understanding needed here |
| --- | --- | --- |
| news should first be split into event and interpretation | This reduces the mistake of copying article wording directly as a conclusion. | It is enough to understand that the announcement, lawsuit, or publication itself is different from its meaning. |
| reports should be checked through methodology and sample | This prevents numbers from being trusted just because they look precise. | It is enough to understand that the institution, scope, and sample should be inspected. |
| columns are viewpoint material, not the whole body of evidence | This reduces confusion between opinion and fact. | It is enough to understand that columns offer framing and questions, but do not replace the whole conclusion. |

## First Separate Event from Interpretation

The first thing to do when reading news is to split `fact event` from `interpretation`.

> event:  
> a company announced a product  
> an institution published a report  
> a lawsuit was filed
>
> interpretation:  
> this announcement will change the industry  
> this lawsuit will stop AI training  
> this technology will damage education

The event is checked through the date and the actors. The interpretation is checked through who is making the claim and on what basis.

## Read Reports Through Methodology

A `report` is more systematic than a news article, but it still should not be copied directly into a conclusion.

| What to check | Question |
| --- | --- |
| issuing institution | is it a government body, research institute, company, or consulting firm? |
| scope of the study | what countries, industries, or time periods were covered? |
| sample | who was asked, and how representative is the sample? |
| metric | what exactly was measured? |
| limit | what limitations did the report itself admit? |

For example, the WEF `Future of Jobs Report` is useful because it analyzes job and skill changes through employer surveys and industry outlooks. But it still does not determine the career outcome of a particular individual.

Public-sector materials such as the U.S. Department of Education's 2023 AI report should be read the same way. They are useful for seeing institutional concern and recommendation direction, but they should not be generalized mechanically to every classroom and learner.

## Use Columns as Viewpoint Material

A `column` is better treated as viewpoint material than as raw evidence. A strong column can clarify a controversy, but it is not the whole evidence base by itself.

> what a column can provide:  
> a frame for the dispute  
> policy concern  
> social reaction  
> critical questions
>
> what a column alone should not determine:  
> the real level of technical performance  
> the final result of a legal judgment  
> the confirmed outcome of market change  
> a generalized conclusion about educational effect

In a learning document like this, a column should be introduced as:

> one author interpreted the issue in this way from this perspective

## Read Different Sources in Layers

AI forecasts should not be judged through one source alone. At a minimum, it helps to layer them like this:

> policy document:  
> what is being regulated or recommended?
>
> research report:  
> what indicators are changing?
>
> news report:  
> what real event or conflict happened?
>
> column:  
> how is that event being interpreted?

Sometimes different sources point in the same direction. Sometimes they conflict strongly. If they differ, the book should not force them into one conclusion.

## The View to Keep from This Section

Reading AI material is not mainly about reaching a quick conclusion. It is about classifying the nature of the material first.

> in news, separate event from interpretation  
> in reports, check methodology and limits  
> use columns as viewpoint material  
> in policy documents, check scope and timing of application  
> read different sources together in layers

## Checklist

- You can separate event from interpretation in a news article.
- You can check the issuing institution, scope, sample, metric, and limit of a report.
- You can use a column as viewpoint material rather than as pure factual evidence.
- You can distinguish obligation from recommendation in a policy document.
- You can show differences when sources conflict.

## When to Recall This View First

- When someone is about to draw an industry-wide conclusion from a single news article
- When a report's number looks persuasive enough to skip checking methodology
- When the viewpoint of a column needs to be separated again from the actual event

In those cases, separate `event`, `interpretation`, `methodology`, and `viewpoint material` first.
