# P2-4.2 Rate of Change and Slope

> Section ID: `P2-4.2`
> Version: `v2026.07.09`

P2-4.1 reopened differentiation as a question about change. This Section narrows that question into a more calculable form: rate of change and slope.

## Scope of This Section

This Section introduces `rate of change`, `slope`, `average rate of change`, and `instantaneous rate of change`. It prepares the bridge toward derivatives and gradients.

## Terms to Fix First

| Term | Very short meaning | Role in this Section |
| --- | --- | --- |
| rate of change | output change compared with input change | central question here |
| slope | graph-based reading of rate of change | visual interpretation |
| average rate of change | change over an interval | first step from line to curve |
| instantaneous rate of change | change near one point | bridge to derivatives |
| curve | function shape whose rate may vary by interval | reason differentiation is needed |

## What Rate of Change Compares

Rate of change does not ask only how much output moved. It also asks how much input moved to produce that change.

\[
\text{rate of change} = \frac{\text{output change}}{\text{input change}}
\]

## Slope Reads the Same Idea on a Graph

For a line such as `y = 2x + 1`, the slope stays constant.

![Example of a line's slope](../../../assets/part-02/chapter-04/linear-slope-constant-en.svg)

That is why lines are the easiest starting point.

## Curves Change by Interval

For a curve such as `y = x^2`, the rate can differ by location or interval.

![Example of a curve's slope](../../../assets/part-02/chapter-04/curve-slope-changing-en.svg)

This is the key reason average change over a whole interval eventually narrows into instantaneous change near one point.

## Why This Matters for Learning

Later, loss curves reuse the same question:

- at the current point, how sensitive is the loss?
- if we move a little, which way lowers it?

## Perspective to Keep

- Rate of change compares output change with input change.
- Slope is a graphical reading of that comparison.
- A line keeps one slope; a curve may not.
- Average change becomes instantaneous change when we focus on one point.
