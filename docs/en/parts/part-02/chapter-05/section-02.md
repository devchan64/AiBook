# P2-5.2 Distribution, Mean, and Variance

> Section ID: `P2-5.2`
> Version: `v2026.07.09`

P2-5.1 treated probability as a language for uncertainty. Now the question changes again: when many values are gathered together, what should we read first?

## Scope of This Section

This Section introduces `distribution`, `mean`, `variance`, `center`, and `spread` at an entry level. It does not go deep into full probability-density functions or detailed standard-deviation usage.

## Central Question

Why is looking only at the mean not enough once values come in groups?

## Terms to Fix First

| Term | Very short meaning | Role in this Section |
| --- | --- | --- |
| distribution | overall shape of values | first big picture |
| mean | one summary center | starting representative value |
| variance | spread around the mean | reveals what mean alone hides |
| center | representative middle position | question behind mean and median |
| spread | how far values scatter from the center | question leading to variance |

## Read in This Order

1. shape
2. center
3. spread

![Chart for reading shape, mean, and variance together](../../../assets/part-02/chapter-05/distribution-mean-variance-summary-en.svg)

## Same Mean Does Not Mean Same Data

Two groups can share the same mean but still feel very different if one stays close to the center while the other spreads far outward.

![Two groups with the same mean but different variance](../../../assets/part-02/chapter-05/same-mean-different-variance-en.svg)

## Perspective to Keep

- Distribution, mean, and variance are different readings of the same group of values.
- Mean gives center, not the whole story.
- Variance is needed because spread matters.

## Short Check

- Can you explain distribution as the shape of a value group?
- Can you explain mean as a center summary?
- Can you explain why the same mean can still hide different data behavior?
- Can you explain variance as spread around the center?
