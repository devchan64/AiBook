# P2-5.3 Sample, Estimation, and Error

> Section ID: `P2-5.3`
> Version: `v2026.07.09`

After distribution, mean, and variance, another question appears: what if we cannot observe the whole population?

## Scope of This Section

This Section introduces `population`, `sample`, `estimation`, `error`, and `sampling bias` at an entry level. It does not cover full confidence-interval or hypothesis-testing procedures.

## Central Question

If the data we have is only part of the whole, how far can we trust the numbers, and where do we need caution?

## Terms to Fix First

| Term | Core question |
| --- | --- |
| population | what is the whole we want to know about? |
| sample | what observed subset do we actually have? |
| estimation | how do we guess the whole from the part? |
| error | how far can that guess be from the truth? |

## Variation and Bias Are Different

Different samples can produce different estimates. That variation is not the same thing as bias.

![Comparison of sampling variation and sampling bias](../../../assets/part-02/chapter-05/sampling-variation-vs-bias-en.svg)

## Perspective to Keep

- A dataset is usually only part of the world, not the whole world.
- Estimation means reasoning from sample to population.
- Error, variation, and bias should not be collapsed into one idea.

## Short Check

- Can you distinguish population from sample?
- Can you explain estimation as a guess about the whole from the part?
- Can you explain why two different samples may produce different estimates?
- Can you explain why variation and bias are not the same?
