# Part 6. LLMs and Generative AI

> Section ID: `P6-index`
> Version: `v2026.07.21`

Part 6 reads generative AI through LLMs as the representative case. The starting point of this Part is not tokens but generative-AI artifacts. First, it asks what generative AI creates, why LLMs are useful as the central example, and why generation should be read as repeated candidate distributions and selection.

Then the text moves into tokens and embeddings as the input units of text generation, and reads Transformers and GPT as structures that create the next candidate. After that, it connects pretraining, fine-tuning, instruction tuning, and alignment to the formation of response habits, then shows how prompts, RAG, vector databases, tool use, agents, MCP, and harnesses reinforce generated results into usable service structures.

Finally, evaluation, automatic and human review, service constraints, failure response, and a small generative-AI feature flow separate a natural answer from a reviewable service result. Development history and the BERT family are placed after the main current as a background map and comparison axis that keeps the GPT-centered explanation from becoming overgeneralized.

## Questions to Hold in This Part

- How are generative-AI artifacts different from classification labels or numeric predictions?
- Why read LLMs as a central example rather than as all of generative AI?
- Why should LLM generation be read as repeated candidate distributions and selection?
- In what order do tokens, embeddings, Transformers, GPT, and next-token prediction connect?
- What differs between tasks handled by prompts alone and tasks that need RAG, tool use, or agents?
- Why is a natural generated answer different from an operable service result?
